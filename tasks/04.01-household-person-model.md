**Codex review finding: this is more foundational than turning every record into an arbitrary share grant, and it must come first.**

Today `accounts.holderName`, `insurancePolicies.coveredMembers` (an unstructured `text[]`), `insurancePolicies.nominee` and `familyMembers` are **unrelated strings**. A name change or a duplicate name silently breaks the relationship, and nothing can answer "whose income is this?" or "which child does this SSY belong to?"

Introduce a stable person record — reusing and extending `family_members` — and link accounts, holdings, policies, goals, liabilities, nominees and income sources to it. Crucially, **a household person need not be a login**: a spouse or child can be a person without an account, which is what most households actually look like.

This unlocks correctness elsewhere that sharing alone cannot:
- whose income is lost in an insurance scenario (12.2)
- whose senior-citizen 80D limit applies (11.7)
- who owns an asset for tax purposes
- whose credit exposure is being assessed
- which child an SSY or education goal belongs to

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Stable person records extending `family_members`; a person may exist without a user login
- [ ] Accounts, holdings, policies, goals, liabilities and nominees link by person **id**, not name string
- [ ] `insurancePolicies.coveredMembers` migrated from `text[]` to person references
- [ ] Renaming a person never breaks a relationship
- [ ] Optional linkage from a registered user to a household person
- [ ] Existing single-user installs migrate with an implicit self person; no behaviour change
- [ ] Tables updated in `ALL_TABLES`/`USER_TABLES`
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
