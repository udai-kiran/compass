The feature no general shopping app can copy, because it needs data only Compass has: **given a shopping list, which portal should I buy each thing from, and which of my cards should I pay with?**

Three inputs combine:
1. **Basket cost per platform** including delivery fees and minimum-cart thresholds (7.2)
2. **Card reward earn rates** from `reward_entries` and `card_issuer_settings`, via the interface exposed in 1.2
3. **Live card offers** extracted from forwarded deal emails (7.3), with their caps and minimum spends

Output: an itemised recommendation — *"split across BigBasket and Amazon; pay BigBasket with HDFC (10% offer, capped ₹1,500 — you hit the cap at ₹15,000), Amazon with SBI (5% back, no cap). Total effective saving ₹2,180 versus buying everything on one portal with your default card."*

The cap arithmetic is the part that must be exactly right. A 10% offer capped at ₹500 beats a 5% uncapped offer only below ₹10,000 of spend, and getting this wrong produces recommendations that are confidently worse than the obvious choice.

Reward points are valued only at the user's configured redemption rate; an unredeemed point is not a rupee, and treating it as one inflates every recommendation.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Recommendation combines basket cost, earn rates and active offers into one effective cost
- [ ] Offer caps, minimum spends and validity windows applied correctly (unit-tested at the cap boundary)
- [ ] Per-item portal split and per-portal card choice both stated, with the saving itemised
- [ ] Points valued only per configured redemption rate; never silently treated as cash
- [ ] Utilization-aware — does not recommend a card that would breach the user's alert threshold
- [ ] Cards with no configured earn rate handled without distorting the comparison
- [ ] Degrades cleanly when there are no active offers (falls back to 7.2 + rewards only)
- [ ] Recommends, never purchases
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
