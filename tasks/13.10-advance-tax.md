The users Compass has built the most machinery for — those with capital gains — are exactly the ones salaried TDS does not cover, and 234C interest is the surprise that follows.

`capital-gains.ts` already computes realised gains by financial year with FIFO tax lots. Add the liability and schedule on top:

- **Instalments** — 15% by 15 Jun, 45% by 15 Sep, 75% by 15 Dec, 100% by 15 Mar (cumulative)
- **234C** — interest for deferment of an instalment
- **234B** — interest where less than 90% of assessed tax is paid by year end
- **The capital-gains exception** — a gain that arises late in the year is not penalised retrospectively for earlier instalments; it becomes payable from the quarter in which it arose. Getting this wrong produces alarming and wrong numbers.
- **Senior citizens without business income are exempt** from advance tax entirely

Income sources: salary TDS (11.1), realised capital gains, interest income, rental. Reuse the existing scheduler and notification infra so due dates arrive as reminders rather than as a page nobody visits.

Estimates are labelled as estimates. This informs a payment decision; it is not a filed return.

## Implementation notes (round-2 review pass)

- **234B window** — `nowMonth` is passed through UNCLAMPED to `interest234B`;
  the helper itself returns zero before April of the assessment year, and
  clamping at the caller fabricated a month of interest during the FY. The
  March cap stays (worst-case exposure stops accruing at AY end in this model).
- **Deliberate inconsistency, recorded:** the zero-payment worst case assumes
  NO payments are made, while instalments still grant the §234C capital-gains
  timing exception. Statutorily that relief is conditional on the attributable
  tax actually being paid through the remaining instalments or by 31 March.
  The task brief mandates honouring the timing exception, and dropping it would
  make every late-year gain look retroactively penalised — the exact failure
  mode this task calls out — so both behaviours stand; their combination is a
  disclosed modelling simplification, listed in the response's assumptions.
