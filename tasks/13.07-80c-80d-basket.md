**Correction from review:** the earlier claim that Compass "already records every input" was wrong. It records *several* — insurance premiums, EPF contributions, PPF/SSY/NPS accounts, and home-loan principal via `splitInstallments` — but **most are not tax-classified and some are absent entirely**:

- **ELSS is indistinguishable from any other equity mutual fund** — the string "elss" does not appear anywhere in the repo. `assetClass` and `gainsTaxClass` both fail to identify it.
- **A five-year tax-saver FD is indistinguishable from a normal FD** — an FD is only `assetClass = "fd"`.
- **NSC is not modelled at all.**
- Tuition fees, preventive health check-ups and employer NPS have no representation.

So this task is **not** mostly aggregation. Tax-classifying instruments is a prerequisite, and should be treated as the bulk of the work.

- **80C (₹1.5L cap)** — employee EPF, PPF, SSY, ELSS, life-insurance premiums, home-loan **principal** (already computed by `splitInstallments` in `emis.ts`), 5-year tax-saver FD, NSC, children's tuition fees
- **80CCD(1B)** — an additional ₹50,000 for NPS, a **separate bucket** that must not be pooled into the ₹1.5L
- **80CCD(2)** — employer NPS contribution, separate again and salary-linked
- **80D** — health premiums: ₹25,000 self/family, ₹50,000 where parents are senior citizens, plus ₹5,000 preventive health check-up

**The honesty requirement that makes or breaks this feature:** since FY 2023-24 the **new regime is the default**, and under it almost none of these deductions apply. A tracker that nudges a new-regime filer to "use your remaining ₹38k of 80C headroom" is giving actively wrong advice. Headroom prompts must therefore be gated on the user actually being on (or leaning toward) the old regime — which is why 11.4 exists.

Deadline-aware: the value is knowing in November, not on 30 March.
