The third [Comparify](https://comparify.pro/) vertical: compare a ride across Uber, Ola, Rapido and the metro/auto alternative before booking.

The same asymmetry applies as with food delivery — a standalone comparator sees one trip; Compass sees the **pattern**. It already holds every ride in the ledger with merchant normalization, so it can surface what a comparator cannot: *"₹4,100 on cabs last month, 60% of it the same office commute — a monthly metro pass costs ₹1,500."* That recurring-commute insight is worth considerably more than saving ₹18 on tonight's ride.

Fare estimates are inherently unstable — surge moves minute to minute — so estimates must be labelled as estimates with their observation time, and the feature must not pretend to a precision it cannot have. Where live fares are unavailable, the spend-pattern analysis stands on its own and is arguably the better half of the feature.

Pickup and drop locations are sensitive household data: they stay on the instance and are never sent to a model.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Per-trip comparison across available providers, including surge where observable
- [ ] Estimates labelled as estimates with observation time; no false precision
- [ ] Recurring-trip detection from merchant-normalized ledger history
- [ ] Pass/subscription break-even computed against real observed commute frequency
- [ ] Works usefully with no live fare data, on spend analysis alone
- [ ] Location data never leaves the instance and never enters a model prompt
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
