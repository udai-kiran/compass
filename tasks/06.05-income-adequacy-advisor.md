When goals and income do not reconcile, say so directly and quantify the ways out. Most finance apps only ever tell you to spend less; that is one lever of four, and often not the effective one.

Given the shortfall from 5.2, quantify each lever:
- **Extend the timeline** — how much later each goal lands if contributions stay flat
- **Reduce the target** — what target the current surplus actually supports
- **Cut expenses** — the monthly reduction needed, pointed at real categories from existing spending analysis
- **Increase income** — the raise, switch or side income required, expressed as a concrete number and as a % of current income

The income lever is the one Compass is unusually well placed to quantify, because it knows the household's actual numbers: *"a ₹18,000/month increase — about 22% — closes this gap entirely and pulls your house deposit forward by three years."* Delivered as arithmetic and options, never as career instruction, and never moralising about spending.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] All four levers quantified against the real shortfall, in rupees and where relevant %
- [ ] Expense lever points at actual categories from existing spending data, not generic advice
- [ ] Income lever states the required increase in absolute and percentage terms
- [ ] Combinations supported (partial expense cut + partial timeline extension)
- [ ] Presented as options with tradeoffs — no single "you must" recommendation
- [ ] No shortfall degrades to a positive confirmation, not an empty panel
- [ ] Deterministic and unit-tested
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
