The decision that determines whether every other deduction in this phase matters at all — and the one most people make once, by default, and never revisit.

Compute liability under both regimes from the same underlying data: the old regime with the 80C/80CCD/80D basket (11.2), HRA, home-loan interest under 24(b), and standard deduction; the new regime with its own slabs, its higher standard deduction, and almost none of the exemptions. Show the crossover — how much deduction the user would need to claim for the old regime to win.

This drives 11.2's behaviour: once a user's regime is known or inferred, 80C headroom nudges either matter or must be suppressed. A user on the new regime should be told plainly that chasing 80C is pointless for them, which is more useful than a tidy progress bar.

Slabs and limits change most budgets, so they must be **versioned by financial year and data-driven**, never hardcoded inline. A stale rate silently produces confident wrong advice.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Liability computed under both regimes from identical inputs, itemised side by side
- [ ] Crossover point stated — the deduction level at which old overtakes new
- [ ] Slabs, rebates, surcharge, cess and standard deductions versioned per financial year in data
- [ ] HRA and 24(b) home-loan interest included in the old-regime path
- [ ] Result feeds 11.2 so headroom prompts are suppressed for new-regime users
- [ ] Marginal-relief and rebate edge cases around the threshold tested
- [ ] Labelled an estimate; assumptions and the FY basis always visible
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
