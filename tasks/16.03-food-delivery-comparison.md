The second vertical [Comparify](https://comparify.pro/) covers, and one where Compass has an advantage no comparator has: **it already knows what this household spends on food delivery**, because the transactions are in the ledger and the extractor already parses the order emails.

Compare the same order across Swiggy, Zomato and direct restaurant ordering, accounting for the charges that make the headline price misleading — delivery fee, platform fee, packaging, surge, GST, and the real value of any subscription (Swiggy One, Zomato Gold) the household already pays for. A subscription already being paid for changes the answer, and only Compass knows it is being paid for.

The genuinely useful insight here is usually not "order from Zomato tonight" but **"you spent ₹7,400 on food delivery last month across 23 orders, ₹1,900 of it in fees"** — a fact Compass can already almost derive today and does not surface. Pair the per-order comparison with that spending reality, and connect it to the grocery side: for repeat orders, cooking cost is a legitimate third option.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Per-order comparison across platforms including delivery, platform, packaging fees, surge and GST
- [ ] Existing subscription benefits factored in from real ledger evidence of the subscription
- [ ] Monthly food-delivery spend and total fees surfaced from existing transaction data
- [ ] Repeat-order patterns identified from merchant-normalized history
- [ ] Degrades to the spend analysis alone when no live price data is available
- [ ] Informational, never nagging; no moralising about ordering in
