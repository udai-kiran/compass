## High

- **The plan cannot calculate either 234B or 234C with the proposed inputs.** It refers to “actual payment” but defines no advance-tax payment input or persisted payment history, and explicitly makes payment tracking a non-goal ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:38), [TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:108)). Without cumulative payment amounts and payment dates, the API can only show instalments or a clearly labelled zero-payment scenario—not actual interest exposure. Add payment inputs/history, or remove the interest amounts and return scenario-based estimates.

- **The capital-gains exception is directionally right but not correctly specified.** A Q3 gain should indeed create no Q1/Q2 234C interest, but relief also requires the tax attributable to that gain to be paid through the remaining instalments, or by March 31 if none remain. Merely adjusting cumulative gain at each due date does not test that condition. Timing must use the actual realization date: a gain on December 20 is not part of the December 15 obligation despite both being “Q3.” The existing statement exposes `sellDate` on each slice ([capital-gains.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/investments/services/capital-gains.ts:87)), whereas the proposed 13.11 set-off result returns only annual net totals and loses timing. Preserve dated gains/loss allocation through set-off and test gains immediately before and after each due date. See the [official Section 234C text](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Documents/income-tax-act-1961-as-amended-by-finance-act-2025.pdf).

- **The 234C deficit thresholds are wrong for June and September.** The payment schedule is 15%/45%/75%/100%, but Section 234C does not charge interest for the first two instalments where at least 12% and 36%, respectively, has been paid. The plan currently charges against 15% and 45% ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:38)), overcharging taxpayers who paid 12–<15% or 36–<45%. December and March use 75% and 100%. The 3/3/3/1 month factors are otherwise correct, at 1% simple interest per month or part thereof. See the Income Tax Department’s [234C guidance](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/interest-and-fees).

- **The alert threshold is off by a factor of 100.** The statutory threshold is ₹10,000, which is `1_000_000` paise, not `10_000` paise ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:83)). It also applies when liability is **₹10,000 or more**, not only when greater. As written, alerts begin above ₹100 and exclude exactly ₹10,000. The official threshold is confirmed by the [Income Tax Department](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/20117/42998/Advance-Tax_2026-01-20_03-07-13_495aed_en.pdf/42a03004-aef2-b348-ff9e-fb3b81769c8a?t=1774070636364&version=1.0).

## Medium

- **The senior-citizen exemption cannot currently be detected safely.** The legal condition is a **resident** individual aged 60 or more at any time during the relevant FY, with no income chargeable under “Profits and gains of business or profession.” The plan omits residence and the FY-relative age test ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:33)). Compass has DOB ([schema.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/system/schema.ts:32)), but no residence indicator, and `income_kind` contains only salary, interest, dividend, rent, and ambiguous `other`—no business/profession kind ([schema.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/tax/schema.ts:177)). Absence of a business row therefore cannot prove eligibility. Add explicit residency and business/profession classification or a user attestation; otherwise return exemption eligibility as unknown rather than exempt. The condition is confirmed in the [official senior-citizen guidance](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-2).

- **AC4 states the 234B interest base incorrectly.** The `<90%` test determines whether 234B applies, but once triggered, interest is on the amount by which advance tax falls short of the full assessed tax—not merely the gap up to 90% ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/096-advance-tax/TASK.md:102)). For example, paying 89% triggers interest on the remaining 11%, not 1%. It runs at 1% for every month or part of a month from April 1 through assessment, with self-assessment/other payments reducing the base from their payment dates. The scope’s “to date of payment” is therefore only correct for a simple full-payment estimate. See [Section 234B](https://wmstatic-prd.incometaxindia.gov.in/web/guest/w/section-234b-37).

- **The TDS source is correct for salary, but too narrow for assessed tax.** Accepted salary `income_events` are the correct source: payslips derive salary events with `tdsPaise` ([income-events.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/tax/services/income-events.ts:340)), and summaries include only accepted rows ([income-events.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/tax/services/income-events.ts:282)). However, advance tax/assessed tax is reduced by applicable TDS/TCS across all included income, not salary alone. Compass already provides `totalTdsPaise` across accepted kinds ([income-events.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/tax/services/income-events.ts:278)). The plan should either use all accepted TDS or explicitly disclose that non-salary TDS/TCS and tax credits are unsupported.

- **The alert-ledger key dedupes correctly, but the integration is incomplete.** `kind=advance_tax_q1…q4` plus `refKey=fy` produces one row per user/FY/instalment because the unique index is exactly `(userId, kind, refKey)` ([schema.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/modules/system/schema.ts:60)). Underscores are valid because `kind` is unrestricted text, though existing code generally uses kebab-case. However, inserting an `alert_ledger` row alone does not create a notification. The plan needs a daily scheduled evaluator, a defined lead window, a `createNotification` call, boot catch-up, and atomic claim-plus-notification behavior so a notification failure does not leave a permanent dedup tombstone. The current jobs framework also requires registering and classifying every new date-sensitive scheduler.

- **There is no circular dependency with 13.8, but the data contract risks double-counting and tight coupling.** 13.8 does not depend on 13.10, and the effective preferred regime already exists independently in `tax_regime_preferences`. Nevertheless, 13.8’s liability already includes `capitalGainsAfterSetOff` ([TASK.md](/work/personal/compass/tasks/094-regime-comparison/TASK.md:17)); 13.10 then separately consumes capital gains for timing. The plan must define liability both with and without each dated gain so it does not add gains twice. Prefer a pure advance-tax calculator receiving tax-liability snapshots rather than importing the regime-comparison service directly. Both 13.8 and 13.11 remain PLANNING dependencies, so sequencing is a blocker rather than a cycle.

## Low

- **`tax-rules.ts` has due dates, percentages, and the 1% rate, but no advance-tax liability threshold.** It correctly contains FY-specific June 15, September 15, December 15, and March 15 dates with 15/45/75/100 cumulative percentages ([tax-rules.ts](/work/personal/compass/apps/api/src/lib/tax-rules.ts:539)), plus `100` bps/month and a senior-exemption flag. P1’s claim that it will read “dates and thresholds” is inaccurate: the ₹10,000 threshold is absent from the type and data. Add an effective-dated `liabilityThresholdPaise` rather than hardcoding it in the alert job.

- **Rounding rules are unspecified.** Official guidance rounds the tax base used for interest to the nearest ₹100 and interest to the nearest ₹10. The pure calculator should define statutory rounding and add boundary tests; integer paise alone does not settle this.

- **FY 2026-27 naming needs a compatibility note.** The rules table includes FY 2026-27 under “234B/234C,” but the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from that FY and uses corresponding Sections 424/425. The calculation policy is materially unchanged, so this is not a formula blocker, but user-facing notes should avoid implying that the legacy section numbering governs FY 2026-27.
