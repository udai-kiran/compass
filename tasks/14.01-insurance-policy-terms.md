**Codex P1, and it must precede adequacy (12.2): adequacy computed from `sumAssuredPaise` alone is too shallow for Indian health insurance and produces dangerously optimistic conclusions.** A ₹10L floater with a 1% room-rent cap and a 20% co-pay is not ₹10L of usable cover.

The current `insurancePolicies` model has none of: deductible, co-pay, room-rent or ICU limits, disease sub-limits, waiting periods (initial, pre-existing, maternity), restoration benefit, no-claim bonus, employer-versus-personal ownership, network/TPA contacts, exclusions, nominee relationship, or covered-member identity — `coveredMembers` is a bare `text[]`, now linked to real people by 2.1.

Add structured terms, and a **claim-readiness checklist**: policy document present, health card, TPA contact, renewal current, waiting periods elapsed, disclosures complete. The moment a claim is needed is the worst possible time to discover a missing document or an unelapsed waiting period.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Structured terms: deductible, co-pay, room-rent/ICU limits, sub-limits, waiting periods, restoration, NCB
- [ ] Employer-owned versus personal cover distinguished — employer cover ends with the job
- [ ] Coverage linked to person records (2.1), not name strings
- [ ] Claim-readiness checklist with the specific missing artifact named
- [ ] Waiting-period end dates computed and surfaced ahead of time
- [ ] Feeds 12.2 so adequacy reflects **usable** cover, not headline sum assured
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
