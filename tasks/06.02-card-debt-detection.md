**Codex review finding, and the most consequential logic gap identified: Compass cannot tell whether a card statement was actually paid in full.**

It knows `statementReconciliations.totalDuePaise` and `minDuePaise`, `cardDetails.dueDay`, repayment links from `services/inbox.ts`, `transactionLinks` and the cycle logic in `services/cards.ts` — everything needed, and it draws no conclusion. So the allocation engine (5.4) could route surplus into equity at an assumed 12% while the household revolves credit-card debt at 42%, and the shopping optimizer (7.6) could then cheerfully recommend that same card.

**High-interest unsecured debt must be a hard planning constraint**, not one input among many.

Add a statement-payment state machine: unpaid / partially paid / paid in full, with paid amount and date, likely revolving balance, estimated finance charge, lost grace period, minimum-only and overdue warnings, and issuer-level exposure across cards.

`cardDetails` currently lacks APR, cash-withdrawal APR, late fee, GST and interest-free-period terms; those must be added for the finance-charge estimate to mean anything.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Per-statement payment state derived from real repayment links, not inferred from balance alone
- [ ] Revolving balance and estimated finance charge computed; APR and terms added to card details
- [ ] Minimum-only payment flagged distinctly from full payment — the two have opposite consequences
- [ ] Issuer-level and total unsecured exposure surfaced
- [ ] Exposed as a **planning constraint** consumed by 5.4, 7.6 and 13.2
- [ ] Interest-free period and grace-period loss modelled per issuer terms
- [ ] Integer paise; deterministic; unit-tested against worked cycle examples
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
