The highest-risk task in the release: the visibility rule changes from `user_id == me` to `user_id == me OR explicitly shared to a household I belong to`.

This must be **one central guard** composed by services — never per-route ad-hoc checks, because the failure mode is a privacy breach rather than a broken feature.

**The known trap:** eight tables have no `user_id` and inherit scoping through a parent FK — `transaction_splits`, `import_rows`, `budget_lines`, `attachments`, `transaction_links`, `holding_valuations`, `holding_events` (and `users` itself as the identity root). A naive `user_id OR shared` guard silently misses all of them. Each must be resolved through its parent, explicitly and individually tested.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] One `withSharing()` helper; no route performs its own sharing check
- [ ] All 8 parent-scoped tables enumerated and individually covered by tests
- [ ] Property test: a member of household A can never read or write any record of household B, across every module
- [ ] Unshared records remain invisible to other household members (private-by-default holds)
- [ ] Revoking a share or leaving a household immediately removes access
- [ ] Demo session posture unchanged
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
