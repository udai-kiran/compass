Compass tracks policies meticulously — sum assured, premiums, renewal dates, insurer — and never asks the only question that matters: **is it enough?**

The same tracking→advising shift being made for goals, applied to protection. All the inputs already exist:

- **Term life** — Human Life Value against income (5.1): income-replacement need, plus outstanding liabilities (home loan, EMIs from `emi_details`), minus liquid assets, minus existing cover. Dependents come from `family_members`, which already models relationships and education stage — a household with two young children and a 20-year home loan needs a very different number from a single earner with none.
- **Health** — family floater adequacy against medical inflation, which in India runs far ahead of CPI. A ₹5L floater bought in 2015 is not a ₹5L floater today.
- **Gaps worth naming** — no personal accident cover, no critical illness, employer-only health cover (which vanishes with the job, exactly when it is most needed).

Report the gap as a number with its workings shown, not a score. And be willing to say cover is adequate — an adequacy check that always finds a shortfall is just a sales pitch.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Term-life gap computed from income, dependents, liabilities and existing cover, with the calculation shown
- [ ] Dependents sourced from `family_members`, including education stage where relevant
- [ ] Health cover assessed against a configurable medical-inflation assumption, surfaced not hidden
- [ ] Employer-only health cover flagged as a continuity risk
- [ ] Explicitly reports "adequate" when it is — no manufactured shortfall
- [ ] Suggests cover **amounts and types**, never insurers or named products (carries the 4.2 rule)
- [ ] Degrades gracefully with no income data or no dependents recorded
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
