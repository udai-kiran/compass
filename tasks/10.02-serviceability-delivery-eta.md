**Basket arbitrage (7.2) produces invalid answers without this.** The cheapest platform is worthless if it does not deliver to your address, or if it delivers in three days when you needed milk this evening. Live comparators in this space — [Comparify](https://comparify.pro/) among them — treat serviceability and delivery time as first-class alongside price, and Compass must too.

Model, per source and per delivery location:
- **Serviceability** — does this platform deliver to this pincode at all
- **Delivery ETA** — the 10-minute quick-commerce promise versus next-day versus scheduled
- **Delivery fee and minimum-cart threshold**, which already feed 7.2's optimizer
- **Slot availability** for platforms that schedule rather than dispatch immediately

Serviceability is what makes the optimizer honest: it must **exclude** non-serviceable platforms from a split rather than recommending an unreachable saving, and it must let the user weigh ₹40 saved against a two-day wait rather than silently optimising for price alone.

The delivery address is household data and stays local to the instance — it is never sent to a model, consistent with the redaction posture everywhere else.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Serviceability tracked per source per delivery location; non-serviceable platforms excluded from arbitrage
- [ ] Delivery ETA band recorded and shown next to price, so speed and cost are weighed together
- [ ] Delivery fee and minimum-cart threshold shared with the 7.2 optimizer rather than duplicated
- [ ] Unknown serviceability is surfaced as unknown, never assumed to be available
- [ ] Stale serviceability data marked with its observation time, as prices are
- [ ] Delivery address never leaves the instance and is never included in a model prompt
- [ ] typecheck + lint + test green
